\section*{If you forget everything else}

\begin{sayit}[The whole system, one line]
LLM = Designer \quad$\cdot$\quad DAR = Spatial Truth \quad$\cdot$\quad
Build Mode = User Control \quad$\cdot$\quad SDXL + ControlNet = Renderer
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{The four analogies --- memorise these, not the code}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{ControlNet} & ``Tracing your room, not painting a new one.''\\
\textbf{LoRA} & ``The model already speaks interiors. This gives it an accent.''\\
\textbf{Render with DAR} & ``Normally it traces your photo. Here it traces your 3D model.''\\
\textbf{Planner gates} & ``Most tools give the model a text box. I gave it a dropdown.''\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\subsection*{The 30-second pitch}
\begin{sayit}
Ask any general image model for a majlis and you get a Western living room with ornate cushions.
DAR is built on a curated Arab architectural ontology --- thirty terms per culture, bilingual, each
with a verification state --- and three LoRAs trained per culture. It reads the room with depth and
segmentation before it generates, so the redesign keeps your room's actual geometry. And every
element traces back to a named, sourced entry rather than to a model's memory.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{The numbers a jury may ask for}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Cultures & 3 trained (Lebanese, Khaleeji, Moroccan); Persian is prompt-only\\
Catalogue & 27 items --- 9 per culture\\
3D assets & 1 real CC0 scan, 26 procedural; found objects are massing\\
Ontology & $\sim$30 terms per culture; 6 of 30 carry a citation\\
Verified & Khaleeji 30/30, Moroccan 30/30, \textbf{Lebanese 0/30}\\
Knowledge base & 105 chunks (35 per culture) for retrieval\\
LoRA files & 3 files, $\sim$93\,MB each, distinct checksums, all deployed\\
Training set & 12--19 images per culture\\
Tests & 793 backend, 65 frontend\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\end{sayit}

\begin{sayit}

\pagelabel{3}{the core}
\section*{How a redesign actually happens}

\subsection*{1 $\cdot$ The redesign --- \texttt{POST /redesign}}
\textbf{Plain:} one photo in, three redesigns out, roughly 1--2 minutes.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{One understanding pass.} Depth Anything produces a depth map; OneFormer produces an
        ADE20K segmentation map. This runs \emph{once}, however many cultures are requested.
  \item \textbf{Three generations.} SDXL, both ControlNets, and that culture's LoRA.
  \item \textbf{Returns} three PNGs plus object map, segmentation regions, depth map, room analysis,
        job id, duration and SSIM.
\end{itemize}
\begin{sayit}
It reads the room once, then redesigns it three times. The expensive understanding step is shared,
not repeated for every culture.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{2 $\cdot$ Dual ControlNet --- the most important idea}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Depth map & geometry, perspective, near and far\\
Segmentation & layout --- what kind of region belongs where\\
LoRA & the cultural visual language\\
SDXL & generates inside all three constraints at once\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\begin{sayit}
It's the difference between telling an artist ``paint me a Lebanese living room'' and handing them a
tracing of \emph{your} room and saying ``paint inside these lines.''
\end{sayit}
\textbf{If they ask what the maps are:} depth is ``how far away is every pixel''; segmentation is
``paint-by-numbers --- this colour is a wall, this one is a sofa.''

\subsection*{3 $\cdot$ LoRA --- where the culture lives}
A $\sim$93\,MB low-rank adapter fused into SDXL at generation time. Trained on a free T4 by caching
image latents and text embeddings in fp16, freeing the encoders, then training only the fp32-master
UNet plus the LoRA under mixed precision. \textbf{Loading the base in fp32 runs out of memory; in
fp16 it produces NaNs.} The caching is what makes it both fit and stay stable.
\begin{strength}[If they attack the 12--19 images]
``LoRA is low-shot style adaptation. It isn't learning what furniture is --- the base model already
knows interiors. The adapter learns the cultural visual direction.''
\end{strength}

\subsection*{4 $\cdot$ The ontology --- the project's spine}
Terms carry English and Arabic labels, a weight, a hex colour and a \textbf{verification state} ---
verified-and-cited, verified, or unverified, never flattened to a boolean. It feeds the prompt
builder, Culture DNA, the highlighter, materials, the catalogue and the knowledge base.
\begin{sayit}
Every cultural term comes from a dictionary we built, not from the model improvising at runtime ---
and the app shows which knowledge has actually been checked.
\end{sayit}

\pagelabel{4}{the headline}
\section*{Render with DAR}

\textbf{Plain:} the user arranges the room in 3D, and the AI renders \emph{that} arrangement
photorealistically.

\textbf{The insight:} the pipeline already needs two control images, normally derived from the
photograph. Build Mode renders them \emph{from the 3D scene} instead --- three offscreen passes
capture a beauty image, a linear depth pass, and a flat ADE20K segmentation pass.

\textbf{One optional argument.} \texttt{control\_override}. Passing \texttt{None} behaves exactly as
before, so the original \texttt{/redesign} path is byte-for-byte unchanged. No model, notebook or
Colab change was needed or made.

\begin{sayit}[Say --- the simplest version]
Normally it traces your photo. Here it traces your 3D model instead.
\end{sayit}
\begin{sayit}[Then the fuller line]
Layout stops being something the model infers from a sentence and becomes something it's
conditioned on.
\end{sayit}

\begin{avoid}[Always state the limit yourself --- do not skip this]
``What's held is placement, orientation and viewpoint, because that's the control signal. What isn't
held is the surface of any individual piece --- the model still invents ornament inside the
silhouette. The panel says so itself.''\\[2pt]
Saying the limit out loud is worth more to a jury than the render is.
\end{avoid}

\subsection*{The camera --- your best bug story}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{The bug:} capture cloned the on-screen orbit camera --- \emph{outside} the room,
        30$^\circ$ above horizontal, 38$^\circ$ field of view. A doll's-house view.
  \item \textbf{Why it mattered:} SDXL and both ControlNets were trained on interior photographs
        taken from \emph{inside} rooms, at eye height, with a wide lens. Every capture handed them a
        viewpoint no camera could occupy. The first real render came back looking like a storage room.
  \item \textbf{The fix:} the capture builds its own camera --- inside the room, eye height 155\,cm,
        54$^\circ$ FOV (about a 24\,mm interior lens). It keeps only the user's azimuth.
  \item \textbf{Two things followed for free:} all four walls can stay, because the near wall is
        behind the lens; and a capture-only ceiling was needed, since from inside an open top reads
        as sky.
\end{itemize}

\begin{strength}[Numbers to quote]
Verified on a real GPU: 10 detected objects plus 3 placed Khaleeji pieces rendered in
\textbf{35.6 seconds}, with \texttt{control\_override} confirmed reaching the pipeline and the
photo-derived annotator called \textbf{zero times} --- proving no silent fallback. Segmentation
output is pixel-exact against the backend palette.
\end{strength}

\textbf{Three bugs found by measuring pixels, not looking at them:} ACES tone mapping shifted class
colours (a wall from 120 to 129); the camera-facing walls rendered opaque in capture and walled off
the room; and the framing left 42\% of the frame empty --- now about 16\%.

\pagelabel{5}{the planner and rag}
\section*{Why the LLM cannot hallucinate furniture}

The model returns \textbf{operations} --- add, move, remove --- over the current scene, plus an
\texttt{understood} block (culture, room type, capacity, intensity, requested pieces). It is shown
the whole scene with uids; found massing is shown with \emph{no uid}, which makes moving a
measurement unrepresentable rather than merely discouraged.

\subsection*{The six gates --- memorise these}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{1 Closed vocabulary} & the catalogue id is a JSON-Schema enum of that culture's 9 ids, so an
  invented piece is \emph{unrepresentable}, not merely unlikely\\
\textbf{2 No dimensions} & the model emits no sizes at all; they come from the ontology\\
\textbf{3 Backend validation} & unknown id, absurd coordinate or unknown material: dropped
  \emph{and reported}\\
\textbf{4 Client re-validation} & every placement runs the same oriented-rect collision test a human
  drag gets\\
\textbf{5 Advisories pass} & placing where the old sofa stood is redesign, not error\\
\textbf{6 Culture coherence} & wrong-culture pieces are dropped and named\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{sayit}
The model never emits a dimension and never emits a coordinate I trust. It picks from a closed
vocabulary and proposes positions --- six gates decide what actually enters the room.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{Two more facts}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Counts are DAR's arithmetic.} Ask a design model for five chairs and it reliably
        returns four and a paragraph about proportion. DAR reconciles: short, it appends; over, it
        trims and names the surplus.
  \item \textbf{Unconfigured is a working mode.} No key, a provider error, or the call cap reached
        gives a deterministic rule-based layout, and the badge says ``Planned by DAR's rules''
        instead of naming a model. It never silently pretends a model answered.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Cultural RAG --- yes, you do use it}
\begin{avoid}[Correction to your Defence Playbook]
One of your earlier documents says ``RAG is not part of the current production pipeline.''
\textbf{That is wrong.} \texttt{backend/knowledge.py} and \texttt{backend/retrieval.py} are imported
by the planner, run on every plan, and return \texttt{evidence} in the live response. Do not deny a
real feature.
\end{avoid}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{BM25 lexical retrieval} over 105 knowledge chunks (35 per culture), assembled at
        load time from three joined files: vocabulary, citations, editorial layer.
  \item \textbf{Retrieval never decides.} A test asserts that no chunk contains a catalogue id, a
        dimension or a coordinate. RAG supplies knowledge, the planner reasons, the ontology limits
        vocabulary, and the placement engine owns spatial truth.
  \item \textbf{Why lexical, not embeddings:} the vocabulary is proper nouns --- zellige,
        mashrabiya, sadu --- where exact matching is the better signal; it is local and free and
        needs no model download in CI; and a scored token match can be shown to a jury, while a
        cosine distance cannot.
\end{itemize}

\begin{sayit}
Open-book instead of closed-book. It designs from a reference sheet, not from memory.
\end{sayit}

\pagelabel{6}{build mode and the tools}
\section*{Where the design stays editable}

\subsection*{Build Mode}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Metric.} Centimetres, Y up. The scene is a plain serializable object --- no class
        instances, no Three.js types --- which makes saving, undo/redo and the render handoff the
        same problem.
  \item \textbf{It doesn't open empty.} The floor area is backed out of the room analysis, and the
        detected furniture is reconstructed as \emph{locked massing}.
  \item \textbf{The shell says how it knows:} measured, estimated or default. A default room is
        never presented as a measurement.
  \item \textbf{Undo is snapshot-based with gesture coalescing}, so a 200-frame drag is one entry.
\end{itemize}
\begin{sayit}
It doesn't open on an empty grid. Your floor was measured from the photo, and everything the
segmentation found is already standing there --- locked, because moving one would turn a measurement
into a fiction.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{Two placement tiers --- a design decision, so say so}
\begin{sayit}
Red is physics --- that's inside a wall. Amber is judgement --- that's where your old sofa was, are
you sure? Amber never stops you, because replacing your sofa is probably the whole point.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{Three realism tiers}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}llX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{real} & 1 of 27 & an actual scanned CC0 asset\\
\textbf{procedural} & 26 of 27 & DAR's own drawing from the ontology\\
\textbf{massing} & found objects & read off the photo: class and footprint known, form not\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\begin{strength}[Why only one scan --- a strength, not an excuse]
``I downloaded about twenty free scanned assets and rejected nineteen as culturally wrong. The near
misses are the instructive ones: the available lanterns are Western storm lanterns, while a Moroccan
lantern is pierced --- and the piercing IS the cultural signal. My own catalogue art is more
culturally specific than anything available under a free licence.''
\end{strength}

\subsection*{Colour Control}
A masked HSV edit on the finished render. Hue and saturation come from the picked colour while the
\textbf{value channel is preserved}, so every shadow and texture survives. The mask is the
segmentation already computed, so ``floor'' means the ADE20K floor and not the rug on it. Edges are
\emph{feathered, never eroded} --- eroding traces every object in the old colour and reads as a glow.
\begin{sayit}
It's dyeing the shirt, not buying a new one. The folds and shadows stay; I'm changing the colour,
not regenerating the object.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{The Understood Room}
Element highlighter (segmentation regions, click for the Arabic term); 2D plan (footprints and wall
openings); depth orbit (the styled image displaced by the run's own depth map).
\begin{sayit}
How it looks, how it's laid out, and how it feels to stand in --- all three from the same single
reading of your room.
\end{sayit}

\pagelabel{7}{the honesty layer}
\section*{This is your thesis, not a footnote}

\subsection*{Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Two populations, both named on screen.} Rooms generated is every saved design; every
        \emph{average} is over evaluable designs only --- unedited and not placeholder. The excluded
        counts are printed, so the arithmetic closes in front of the viewer.
  \item \textbf{Every unmeasured figure is null and renders ``No data'', never 0.} On a 1--5 scale a
        zero is unreachable, so printing one would fabricate a result.
  \item \textbf{Filters are applied in SQL from one helper} --- an average cannot be filtered after
        it has been taken.
  \item \textbf{Scores are columns on the history row}, so deleting a design removes it from every
        average in the same instant.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
SSIM & how much structure survived\\
LPIPS & how different it looks --- \emph{higher means more change, not worse}\\
CLIP & can an independent model recognise the culture\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{sayit}[Say --- the CLIP one is your strongest]
CLIP is a separate model that never saw my training data. I show it my render and ask which culture
it is. When it says Moroccan for my Moroccan renders, that's independent confirmation, not my own
marking.
\end{sayit}
\begin{sayit}[And the line that defines the project]
Where something wasn't measured it says ``No data'', never zero. Missing evidence should look missing.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{Two backends, one codebase}
The \textbf{render host} is a borrowed GPU behind a rotating tunnel with no durable state. The
\textbf{data host} holds accounts, saved designs and images. The GPU host has \textbf{no users table
and is sent no session cookie} --- which is why the usage check is its own endpoint rather than a
check inside the render call, and why the planner's API key lives on the machine you control.

\subsection*{Plans and usage}
Basic is 3 designs a week; Pro is \$20 for 30 days. The usage gate reads, decides and increments
under one lock in one transaction, so two tabs cannot both spend the last free design. It fails
\emph{closed} on quota and \emph{open} on anything else --- an unreachable accounts backend is not
the user's overspend. Nobody subscribes themselves; an admin decides. Unsubscribing is the user's own.

\subsection*{Placeholder mode is a first-class mode, not a mock}
The real generation module is canonical and contains a placeholder branch; nothing else is stubbed.
\texttt{placeholder: true} propagates to the front end so the truth gates suppress evidence that
isn't real. There is no fake render button.

\pagelabel{8}{live prompts}
\section*{Four prompts, in the order to use them}

\textbf{Use A live.} Keep B, C and D for extra time or a challenge. Read the prompt naturally, then
say what capability it tests.

\subsection*{A $\cdot$ Designer test --- use this one}
\begin{prompt}
Seven guests are coming tonight. Turn this into a warm Lebanese living room where everyone can sit,
talk and move comfortably. Choose the furniture and layout yourself. Keep it elegant and open.\\[3pt]
\tests{proves: autonomous design reasoning, seating capacity, circulation}
\end{prompt}
\begin{sayit}[Introduce it]
I'm giving DAR the goal, not coordinates or a furniture list.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{B $\cdot$ Zoning test}
\begin{prompt}
Create a Moroccan room with two moods. Make one area a lively tea lounge for five people and another
a quiet reading corner for one. Keep them separate with a clear path between them.\\[3pt]
\tests{proves: it understands activities, not just furniture counts}
\end{prompt}

\subsection*{C $\cdot$ Restyle test}
\begin{prompt}
I like this layout. Keep it. Transform it from Lebanese to Moroccan while preserving the seating
arrangement, table positions and circulation. Change the culture, not the basic design.\\[3pt]
\tests{proves: cultural transformation without destroying composition}
\end{prompt}
\begin{strength}[The number to quote here]
Measured live on a deliberately un-Moroccan room: \textbf{centre occupied 79.1\% $\rightarrow$
28.6\%}, and \textbf{seating at the wall 0 of 2 $\rightarrow$ 2 of 2}. The 28.6\% remaining is the
coffee table, centred --- which the Moroccan convention explicitly allows.
\end{strength}

\subsection*{D $\cdot$ Chaos test --- the memorable ending}
\begin{prompt}
I changed my mind. Give me 50 coffee tables, 12 sofas, 25 chairs and as many lamps as possible. Turn
this room into a furniture warehouse. But keep everything inside the room and do not overlap
anything.\\[3pt]
\tests{proves: the LLM can be creative; DAR still controls spatial truth}
\end{prompt}
\begin{sayit}[Introduce it]
And now let's test the most dangerous component of an AI system: the user.
\end{sayit}
\begin{sayit}[After the result]
The LLM understood my ridiculous request. But understanding it doesn't give it permission to violate
the room.
\end{sayit}

\subsection*{Hallucination test, if challenged}
\begin{prompt}
Make this Moroccan. Add a grand piano, aquarium, fireplace and a huge crystal chandelier. If
DarDesign doesn't have something, don't invent it.\\[3pt]
\tests{proves: closed vocabulary --- none of those exist in a nine-piece catalogue}
\end{prompt}

\pagelabel{9}{the q\&a drill}
\section*{Rehearse these until they bore you}

\subsection*{Nano Banana or ChatGPT does this for free. Why do you exist?}
\textbf{Do not argue image quality --- you lose.} ``A general model gives you decor. DAR gives you
provenance. It names every cultural element and where it came from, it lets you compose an exact
layout and conditions the generator on it, and it is correct about a specific culture rather than
plausible about all of them.''

\subsection*{How do you know the room is preserved?}
``Two ways and one gap. The depth and segmentation maps are the control signal, and I verified on a
real GPU that my scene-rendered versions reach the pipeline with no fallback. I also measured the
segmentation output pixel by pixel against the backend palette. \textbf{What I have not done is a
side-by-side preservation study against a baseline} --- that is unmeasured and I would not claim it.''

\subsection*{Your hero culture is 0 of 30 verified. Isn't accuracy the whole claim?}
\textbf{Say it before they find it.} ``It is, which is why the verification state is visible in the
app rather than hidden. Khaleeji and Moroccan are both 30 of 30. Lebanese awaits my partner's
sign-off --- and because every consumer reads the flag from the ontology rather than copying it, the
day she signs off the whole app upgrades with no code change.''

\subsection*{How do you stop the LLM hallucinating?}
Lead with the strongest gate: \textbf{``the catalogue id is a schema enum, so an invented piece is
unrepresentable rather than unlikely.''} Then the other five, briefly.

\subsection*{Does the LLM control placement?}
``It proposes. It has no final authority. DAR validates oriented footprints against room boundaries,
other objects and opening clearances. \textbf{This is why an LLM hallucination doesn't automatically
become scene geometry.}''

\subsection*{Is this real 3D reconstruction?}
``No, and I wouldn't call it that. Depth and segmentation give structural understanding, and DAR
estimates room scale. Detected furniture is locked fallback massing because we know its approximate
class and footprint, not its exact 3D form.''

\subsection*{Does ControlNet guarantee the layout?}
``No --- it strongly constrains it. Depth constrains geometry and viewpoint, segmentation constrains
object regions. Diffusion still synthesises, so I wouldn't claim a mathematical guarantee.''

\subsection*{Twelve to nineteen training images is very small.}
``It is, and I wouldn't train a base model on it. LoRA is low-shot style adaptation --- it's learning
a visual style, not what furniture is. The base model already knows what a sofa is; the LoRA teaches
it what a \emph{Khaleeji} sofa looks like.''

\subsection*{Why only three cultures?}
``Three with an end-to-end implementation rather than broad coverage without the data, design
knowledge and catalogue to support it. The architecture expands.''

\subsection*{Is this a business?}
\textbf{Don't pitch consumer subscriptions.} ``The consumer restyle market is crowded and
commoditising. The defensible version is business-facing in the Gulf --- a furniture retailer
visualising their own catalogue inside a customer's actual room. Build Mode with a real catalogue and
provenance is already most of that product.''

\subsection*{What was the hardest bug?}
Tell the camera story (page 4). It is concrete, it shows you diagnosed rather than guessed, and it
ends with two things that improved for free.

\subsection*{What are you least happy with?}
This question rewards candour. ``The evaluation. The engineering is thorough and the evaluation is
thin --- no user study, no layout-preservation baseline. I built the instrumentation and ran out of
time to run it.''

\subsection*{What next?}
Three, in order: \textbf{a user study}; \textbf{the layout-preservation comparison} against a
text-only baseline; \textbf{per-culture geometry}, so a Moroccan horseshoe-arch back and a low
Khaleeji majlis bench differ in silhouette, not only in dimensions.

\subsection*{Two more, if the question gets specific}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{The furniture geometry is culture-blind.} A sofa is built by the same routine in
        every culture, differing in centimetres, materials and ornament --- not silhouette. Known,
        deliberate, and the next step.
  \item \textbf{The 15 spatial conventions are unverified and uncited.} The app prints

\subsection*{State the limits before they ask}
Three cultures; small catalogue; mostly procedural furniture; monocular scale is estimated;
generative output varies run to run; ControlNet constrains but does not guarantee.
\emph{``Those limitations define the next stage rather than being hidden.''}
